# Going Off-Script & Failure States
*Players are players. Here's how to keep the arc alive when they refuse the hook, befriend the villain, defy the Devil, or lose a fight — without railroading. The throughline is robust because **two forces both want the party pointed at Lyssa: the Keepers (openly) and Strahd (secretly).** If one push fails, the other still works.*

---

> [!warning] ✂️ Larnak is cut — 19 July 2026
> The two Larnak branches below are **obsolete** (he never appears; there is no contract to refuse and no spy to kill). Kept for reference/sequels. The party's motivation now runs on the "— L." note, the retreat trail, and Donovich's plea — if they balk at going underground, Urwin's line still works: *"The Keepers pay their debts. Live to collect."* The deeper point of the old branch still holds: **refusing every hook changes nothing** — every road in the valley points at Lyssa, and that's the horror.

## Branch — "We're not working for that snake." *(refusing Larnak — OBSOLETE)*
The Larnak job is a *convenience*, not the only road. The party already has every reason to go to the old churchyard without him:
- **The Keepers point them there anyway.** [[Urwin Martikov]] has the same intel (the digs, the Bluetspur signature); he'll ask them to finish what the siege started — for the valley, not for gold.
- **Personal stakes.** If Sef died (or villagers fell), the party wants Lyssa regardless of who's paying.
- **Larnak shrugs and leaves.** He doesn't *need* them to take his coin — only to go. He'll say so, smiling, and melt off. (This actually *strengthens* the twist: they go on their own terms and are still doing exactly what Strahd wanted.)

> [!tip] Let refusing him feel smart, not punished. The point of the betrayal is that distrusting Larnak changes *nothing* — which is the horror.

## Branch — "Let's deal with Larnak." *(killing/exposing the spy — OBSOLETE, see above)*
- He's a **Spy (CR 1)**; a determined party can take him. Let them — but he **knows almost nothing.** Under threat or *Zone of Truth* he gives up only: he was hired by "a patron's man" to point capable folk at Berez; he's never met his employer; the coin is real. He genuinely believes he's a middleman. Killing him removes a guide, not the plot.
- **Strahd simply sends another nudge** — a Vistani "rumour," a Keeper's lead, a second letter. The road stays open.

## Branch — "What if we side with Lyssa?" *(parley with the villain, Session 3)*
This is a *great* table to run. Lyssa is happy to "recruit" capable outsiders against Strahd.
- **She offers:** safe passage, a share of Barovia "when the Count is gone," even a Dark Gift or her protection. She means roughly none of it — the party are tools to her as much as to Strahd.
- **The catch:** her plan needs them to *test or extend her servants* (fight something worse, deliver the Bluetspur talisman, betray the Keepers). Siding with her means turning on Urwin's people.
- **Where it goes:** if they genuinely ally, Lyssa survives the session and becomes the patron of a darker continuation — but Strahd now has *two* rivals to play against each other, and the Keepers become hunters of the party. A bleak, fascinating branch; flag to the table that it's a turn toward villainy.
- **The likely real outcome:** they hear her out, she shows her contempt, and the parley curdles into the fight. Either way it's a richer scene than a door-kick.

## Branch — "We try to defy / expose / kill Strahd." *(player rebellion)*
- **He cannot be beaten** by a Level 6–8 party; engaging him properly is a 50-session campaign. He is **climate, not boss** — see [[Strahd von Zarovich (the unseen hand)]].
- If they hunt him, he **lets them feel powerful for a moment, then makes escape impossible and the lesson clear** — he toys, withdraws, and does *not* kill them (he needs them; see below). 
- **Exposing him** to the Keepers changes little: the Keepers already know what the Count is. Realising they were his pawns is the *intended* gut-punch, not a plot they can foil. Let the anger be real and impotent — that's Barovia.

## Failure states — when a fight goes wrong
Barovia is deadly by design (those CRs are high). Build in soft floors so a loss is a *cost*, not a dead campaign.

### Near-TPK — the Strahd safety net *(the elegant one)*
> [!gmonly]
> **Strahd will not let his instruments die before they've done his work.** If the party is about to be wiped *before Lyssa is dealt with*, the **Mists intervene** — a sudden fog swallows the fight, and the party wakes elsewhere (the Keepers' church, the woods), alive, wounded, stripped of nothing but a little dignity… and certainty. No one saw who pulled them out. *(In hindsight, the Farewell letter's "the Mists are not always mine to call off" implies exactly who did.)* This reinforces the twist instead of breaking the story. Use it **once.**

### Losing the church *(Session 1 — didn't happen; the church held cleanly)*
Kept for reference: if a siege rout had happened, the Keepers retreat into the **undercroft** and the attackers withdraw at dawn. As played, the party held with room to spare.

### Losing the crypt (Session 2)
If the Harbinger fight collapses: the **wereraven reveal fires** (ravens down through the North Chantry, Urwin unfolds, dawn-light floods the rotunda — the Harbinger withdraws rather than be unmade). If the *remnant* fight somehow goes wrong first, the survivors don't pursue past the false crypts — they're more trapped than the party is. A retreat to the surface costs time, not the arc; the Warden will still be there when they go back down.

### Losing a PC
- A character death should *land* — Barovia earns its losses. But see resurrection below; raising the dead here is hard, which is part of the dread.
- A captured (not killed) PC can be held in Lyssa's churchyard cells — turning the finale into a rescue. Good drama.

### A boss "wins"
- **Alenka/Mirabel/Sorvina (S1)** fight to the death; if they down the party, fall back on the Strahd safety net (he needs them for later).
- **Lyssa always flees** — she's never a TPK risk herself. The **mind flayers / Nosferatu** are the real killers; pull the safety net before a wipe.

## Resurrection in Barovia *(canon — Resurrection Madness)*
> [!gmonly]
> The souls of the dead are **trapped in the Mists** and can't reach the afterlife (full rule: [[Setting Rules — Barovia]]).
> - **Revivify** (within its time limit) works — but the PC **screams on waking**, as from a nightmare. No lasting madness.
> - A humanoid **dead 24+ hours** that's raised gains a **random indefinite madness** (roll on the DMG *Indefinite Madness* table) — the soul's horror at realising it's trapped here, likely forever.
> - **Escape magic fails** (*Teleport, Plane Shift, Wish* to leave) — and if a PC casts **Sending/Commune** to call for help, **Strahd can answer instead.** Lean into both.
> Net effect: a quick Revivify saves a friend with a scream; leaving someone dead overnight brings them back *broken.* Death here is a real reason to get out alive.
