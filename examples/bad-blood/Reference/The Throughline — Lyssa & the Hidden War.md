# The Throughline — Lyssa & the Hidden War
*GM-only. The single spine the whole arc hangs on, and the breadcrumb chain that carries it. Read this once; then you can run any session knowing exactly what each clue is **for**. Player-facing summary: there isn't one — that's the point.*

> [!warning] Updated for the four-session plan — 4 July 2026
> The **tavern is cut** and the sessions remapped (S1 = the siege, S2 = the catacombs, S3 = the descent). The **current escalation ladder and clue chain live in [[Arc Plan — Four Sessions]]** — the versions below are kept for the *why* behind each clue. The spine itself is unchanged.

> [!gmonly] The one-sentence version
> **Everything wrong in the valley is [[Lyssa von Zarovich]]'s doing; [[Strahd von Zarovich (the unseen hand)|Strahd]] noticed, and quietly imported the party to clean it up for him.** ★ *Updated 26 Jul:* the party IS told this — **by Lyssa herself, at the S3 Trap.** She has worked out the frame, is insulted by it, and reveals it truthfully (the Choir confirms it to Lykta). Every session lays a brick; his rival kicks the wall over. **Strahd himself still never appears** — the reveal is her word plus the evidence already in their hands, and the Mists opening at the end is his receipt.

## The spine *(the GM truth, in order)*

[[Lyssa von Zarovich]] — a distant von Zarovich, a vampire nightbringer — means to depose Strahd. She can't beat him head-on, so she wages a quiet war: her agents seek out the valley's **old, half-buried sites of power and deliberately wake the evils sealed inside them.** It does two things at once — it sows chaos that frays Strahd's grip, and it harvests old dark magic to feed her real project: **new servants, built with help from beyond the Mists (Bluetspur), that the Count's ancient tricks can't touch** (the vampire mind flayers).

> [!gmonly] ★ Addendum, 25 July 2026 — the Choir Below (the spine's deepest layer)
> The harvest has a single heart: beneath her sanctuary is **the Choir Below**, a **vestige** her own family imprisoned. The *older Zarovich branch* — the erased names, the sun rosettes, the Tomb-Warden — were never mere rivals of the Count: they were **jailers**, and their consecration was the lock. Lyssa turned the family prison into a battery farm (the green candles are chains; the siphoned power is what makes her servants possible). This is *why* the blood door opens on Lucian (last of the jailers' blood), *why* the voices have called Lykta since the broken span (first ear in fifty years that could hear), and *how* the party deals her a mortal blow in S3 win or lose — **breaking the Binding frees the Choir and guts the project permanently.** Freed, it goes to the **Amber Temple** — where, fifty years on, it is the thing that answers Sef and gives four held souls back. *"We have you"* is the arc's longest-fused line. Full mechanics and truth: [[Session 3]].

Her first strike in the village was the **Blood of the Vine** — Lyssa's courier steered greedy **Sorvina** into digging out the buried **Temple of Ravenkind** beneath the tavern; greed was the lever, the sisters collateral and an early, crude experiment. *(The party never sees this — the tavern is cut. Its clue-work now travels through Urwin's "working down a list" lore and the "— L." orders.)* What the party **does** see is her second, bolder move: the **field test on the Keepers' church** — the siege of Session 1.

**Strahd is not the one who set the party in motion for any noble reason — he simply notices the pattern.** Old sleeping horrors waking one after another, all across his domain, all bearing the same hand: this is a rival moving against him, and he is too proud and too careful to be *seen* striking at family. So he does what he always does — he opens the right door and lets the right strangers wander through it. The **Mists pull the party in** (the bait talisman — his), and his smiling spy **[[Ernst Larnak]]** nudges them down the trail, so that **deniable outsiders cull Lyssa's experiments for him.**

The party survives Lyssa's field-test on the Keepers (**S1**), kills the Tomb-Warden inside her dig (**S2**), and destroys her sanctuary-lab (**S3**). Then Strahd pays the only coin that matters — he **opens the Mists and lets them leave** — his rival routed, his own hands clean. The cruelty isn't that they were trapped; it's that the open door was never freedom. It was a tool being put back in its drawer.

> [!gmonly] Two hands, never confuse them
> - **Lyssa's hand** = the *cause* of the horrors. The woken sites, the corrupted sisters, the mind flayers, the dig at the old churchyard. Signed, where it's signed at all, **"the lady" / "— L."**
> - **Strahd's hand** = the *frame* around the party. The Invitation, the bait talisman, the Mists opening and closing, **Larnak**. Signed, at the very end, **Strahd**.
> The party spends three sessions chasing Lyssa's hand. ★ *26 Jul:* at the Trap, **Lyssa shows them Strahd's hand herself** — being handled as pest control is the insult of her century, and telling them doubles as her recruitment pitch. What was "the glimpse at the door home" is now the villain's own reveal; the door home just confirms it.

## The escalation ladder *(subtle → overt — never get ahead of this)*

**Current, remapped ladder: [[Arc Plan — Four Sessions]].** In brief:

| Session | What the party *feels* | Stays hidden |
|---|---|---|
| **0.5** | "Something is *wrong* with this valley, and it wanted us here." | No villain named at all. Not Lyssa, not Strahd. |
| **1** | "There's a hand behind this, and it signs '— L.'" *(As played: the faction war clocked unaided; the "— L." orders; no Lyssa, no Larnak — both cut.)* | That the party were *aimed* — Strahd's hand around them. |
| **2** | "It was under us the whole time — and we can actually fight back." *(The dig from inside; the manifest; the Tomb-Warden killable; the rune-family whisper.)* | Whose seal that rune-tradition serves; what's below the broken seal. |
| **3** | "We broke her engine — even if it killed us. And we finally know whose errand this was: *she told us.*" | Strahd **in person** (the letter/coach stay cut — the frame is revealed by Lyssa's mouth, never his); what *"we have you"* truly means (detonates in the Long Dark, fifty years on). |

> [!warning] The cardinal rule of the throughline
> **Don't get ahead of the ladder.** The dread comes from the party *assembling* this themselves, one session late each time. *(As played, the ladder shifted: Lyssa's S1 rooftop cameo and S2 naming were cut — she is unseen AND unnamed until the S3 Trap, where she delivers the name and the Strahd-frame in one scene, ★ 26 Jul.)* Strahd is never named as the mover until Lyssa names him at the Trap. When in doubt, under-explain — the notes are written so a quiet clue lands now and *detonates* a session later.

## The clue chain

**The current 10-clue chain (revised for the cut tavern) lives in [[Arc Plan — Four Sessions]]** — planted/pays-off for every breadcrumb: the bait talisman, the rune family, "woken on purpose," the "— L." orders, the faction war, Larnak, the erased family, the non-Barovian mind flayer, Strahd's buried coffin, and the Farewell letter.

> [!tip] If a table wants to *connect the dots out loud*
> Reward it — but make them do the connecting. The pieces are all there: the same elegant hand on the "— L." orders (S1) and the manifest (S2); wolves that hunt her dead but never approach the church; a stranger (Larnak) who always knows where they'll be. A clever party should reach *"Lyssa has been doing this all along, and someone else keeps pointing us at her"* by the end of S2 — which is exactly right. The only thing they shouldn't pin until the letter is **who** the second hand belongs to.
