# Player Pitch — *Bad Blood*

> _A wrong turn in the fog. A valley that doesn't appear on any map. And a long, hungry night that wants to know everything about you._

## The pitch
You're an **established adventuring party** — you already know each other, you travel together, and you've got history. You're on a familiar road, sharing a campfire and a long way still to go. That's all you need to know to start — because sometime in the night, the **Mists** roll in, and the road you knew is gone.

You wake in **Barovia**: a cursed valley of black pines and bruise-coloured skies, where a castle watches from a mountain that never gets closer, the wine is the colour of old blood, and the land itself learned the taste of people a long time ago. Strangers aren't welcome here — but the few who'll talk to you have *work*, and work is the only way anyone survives this place. Pour a drink, take the job, and try to be the kind of people who walk back out of the dark.

This is **gothic horror**: mystery, dread, hard choices, and monsters you can't always out-fight. Bring a character with something to lose.

## The essentials
|              |                                                  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **System**   | D&D 5e (2024 rules)                              |
| **Build at** | **Level 6** (we'll level up over the arc, to ~8) |
| **Length**   | 3 sessions, ~3 hours each                        |
| **Party**    | min 4 player characters                          |
| **Genre**    | Gothic horror — Barovia / Ravenloft              |

## Building on D&D Beyond
**Make a standard 6th-level character.** Don't sweat optimisation — a fun, characterful build beats a perfect one. A few things that'll serve you well here:

- **Expect the undead.** Ghouls, vampires, and worse stalk this valley. **Radiant damage, magic weapons, and a reliable source of healing are worth their weight in gold.** If your character has *any* of these, you'll shine.
- **Cover the bases as a group.** Between the four of you, aim to have: someone who can **heal**, someone who brings **light / radiant**, and someone who can handle **talking and investigating** (this is as much a mystery as a brawl).
- **Mind the nasty conditions.** Vampires and horrors love to **Charm, Frighten, and Paralyse.** Saves, condition removal, or just good positioning will save lives.
- **No special books required.** A core-rules character is perfectly at home here.

## Optional — fits the setting perfectly
If you want a build steeped in the horror of this place, the new **Ravenloft: The Horrors Within** player options are a great match — see **[[Domains of Dread — Index]]** in the vault. Strong thematic fits:
- **Subclasses:** Undead Patron (Warlock), Shadow Sorcery (Sorcerer), Phantom (Rogue), College of Spirits (Bard), Grave Domain (Cleric).
- **Backgrounds:** Haunted One, Investigator.
- **Species:** Dhampir, Reborn, Hexblood, Lupin.
- **Feats / Dark Gifts:** Survivor, Sharp Eye — and the ominous Dark Gifts, if you and the DM want a sinister edge.

## Before session one
You're a party that already knows each other, so we open **around the campfire, the night before everything changes** — come ready to:
- **Re-introduce your character** — name, look, how they carry themselves.
- **Share a bond** — how you know the rest of the party, and one thing you've been through together. (Sort this out as a group beforehand if you can — even a loose "we've travelled together for a year" helps.)
- **Why you're all on this road** — what job, goal, or trouble are you travelling toward *together*?
- **One thing that scares them.** This is a horror game; a fear gives us something to play with. (No real-world phobias unless you're happy to.)

**A note on tone:** there'll be some gore — it's a horror game — but I run it like a **PG-13 film**: creepy and bloody when it counts, never gratuitous. If something ever does land wrong for you, just tell me and we'll ease off, no fuss.

**Questions?** If anything here is unclear — rules, your build, the setting, what's allowed — **just ask me.** Better to sort it before session one than to puzzle over it at the table.

See you in the fog.
