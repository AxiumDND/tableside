# Bad Blood — What Next (Ideas & Reusable Assets)
*The arc is done. This page exists so that when you want to run something else — another three-shot, or full **Curse of Strahd** — you don't start from a blank page. What worked, what's reusable, and six concrete things you could run.*
*Companions: [[Bad Blood — Arc Conclusion & Continuity]] (what happened and what's owed) · [[Tracker — Bad Blood]] (final state) · [[Bad Blood — Overview]] (the arc itself).*

> [!tip] The one-line version
> **The format worked.** Session 0.5 on-ramp + 3 sessions, ~3 hours, 2 combats max, milestone 6→8. Keep it. The two cheapest things to run next are **the tavern you never entered** and **the ending you never got to use** — both are already written.

---

## 1. What three sessions of evidence actually told you

*Not opinions — things the table demonstrated.*

> [!abstract] What this group does
> - **They investigate properly.** The Blood Door puzzle got a full 45 minutes and earned every one of them. Gravestone rubbings, magnifying glass, chalk, familiars sent ahead, the monster notebook. **Give them things to work out, not things to find.**
> - **The best scene of the whole arc had no dice in it** — the Lyssa interrogation. Not "roll persuasion": four players asking a villain hard, specific questions about consequences (*will the villagers be safer? do you have to feed? can you send us home?*). **Build villains who can be interviewed.**
> - **They solve fights sideways.** Ball bearings, Mirror Image, Faerie Fire, Phantasmal Force, a broom used as a firing platform, a bat familiar sent through a gap to scout a coffin. **A boss that only answers to damage will get flattened; a boss with a battlefield they can engineer against is a great fight.**
> - **They refuse binary morality.** *"One vampiric dictator over another is not really a winning situation."* Nobody took the deal. **Don't build a two-door choice — they'll kick a third one.**
> - **They go off-script in ways better than the plan.** Lykta writing to Strahd unprompted is the single best idea anyone had all arc, and it wasn't yours. **Leave holes.**
> - **The horror that landed was atmospheric, not statted.** Whispers through a broken span. Bodies cut in half. Nameplates chiselled blank. Two empty brine jars. The Warden — a big scary stat block — died in five rounds and got a shrug.
> - **Fireball deletes rooms.** Twice. Design encounters assuming one room per session evaporates.

> [!warning] What to fix
> - **Prep-to-play ratio was poor at the end.** Session 3's Binding, the Unchaining, the green passage, the Offer, both engineered spotlight beats — all written, none played. **Roughly half of the final session's prep never reached the table.**
> - **The dungeon crawl ran long.** S2's crypt was ~90 minutes of methodical room-clearing. Good play, but it crowded out the Harbinger fight entirely and pushed the whole arc back a session.
> - **A milestone got missed live.** Level 8 was earned and never called. **Put level triggers on the night sheet as a checkbox, not in prose.**
> - **Named NPCs kept getting cut and then mattering anyway** — Milos, Ernst, Sef. Ernst was cut on 19 July and turned up on the night. **Cut fewer people; give them less to do instead.**

**The design lesson, in one line:** *this table is better at your prep than your prep is at them.* Build **fewer scripted beats and more interactive furniture** — a room with things in it, a villain who can be interviewed, an objective they can work out — and let them cook.

## 3. Six things you could run next

*All are three-shots in the format that worked. Ordered by how little work they need.*

### ★ Pitch A — **The Long Dark**
> *Four strangers claw out of shallow graves. Fifty years have passed. The woman with the spade has been waiting.*

The unplayed TPK epilogue, promoted to a premise. A party wakes in a Barovia five decades on from a disaster nobody will explain, each carrying a **Dark Gift they are never told they have** — the player just notices something is wrong with their senses. The three sessions are working out *what they were*, *who buried them*, and *what the person who dug them up agreed to pay.*

- **Reuses:** the entire M7 script, `Scene - The Long Dark`, `Sef Martikov — Old`, the Dark Gift machinery, the village and church maps, [[Setting Rules — Barovia]].
- **Why it suits this table:** it is a pure investigation premise with a mystery they cannot brute-force, and the hidden-gift mechanic rewards paying attention.
- **Prep needed:** genuinely low. The best scene is already written.

### ★ Pitch B — **Blood o' the Vine**
> *The tavern you never walked into.*

Run [[Tavern — Blood o' the Vine (Backup)]] as its own arc — the three corrupted sisters, Arik behind the bar, the cellar. Expand from mini-adventure to three sessions by making it a **siege from the inside**: the party takes rooms above a tavern that is slowly, politely, eating its guests.

- **Reuses:** the whole backup doc, three floors of printed maps, four portraits, `Scene - The Taproom`, the [[Second Skin]] Dark Gift reward.
- **Why it suits this table:** heavy on social play and investigation, light on corridors. It's the S2 crawl's opposite.
- **Prep needed:** low. Mostly expansion, not creation.

### Pitch C — **The Smiling Man**
> *A well-dressed man is always dry in the rain, and he is not in Barovia any more.*

[[Ernst Larnak]] outside the Mists. Strahd's reach beyond his own domain, or Ernst freelancing after a job went wrong. Set it anywhere — a city, a trade road, a different domain — and make it about the **letter**: someone is being fetched, and the party can work out who and why before the door closes on them.

- **Reuses:** Ernst's sheet and portrait, the Invitation letter device, [[The Mists — Getting In & Out]].
- **Why:** a villain who never fights, is always polite, and is *right about everything he says*. Also the cleanest way to bring the Bad Blood party back without returning to Barovia.
- **Prep needed:** medium — new location, existing antagonist.

### Pitch D — **The Amber Debt** *(direct sequel — same PCs)*
> *Lykta needs something back. There is exactly one place in this valley that gives things back, and it charges.*

The party's stated goal at the end of Session 3, run as an arc: up the mountain to the **Amber Temple**, to restore what the mind flayers took — while the thing already inside Lykta has opinions about going home. Meanwhile Strahd's courtesy has expired.

- **Reuses:** the vestige, the Dark Gift machinery, [[Rewards & Leveling]], every Barovia asset, the refusal cliffhanger.
- **Why:** the players wrote this hook themselves. That is the highest-buy-in premise available to you.
- **Prep needed:** medium-high — the Amber Temple is a real build.

### Pitch E — **The Keepers**
> *Everyone in this village is lying about something, and half of them have feathers.*

A faction/spy three-shot with the **wereraven reveal** as its spine. The Keepers of the Feather have kept a secret through three sessions of siege; make the arc about the party being trusted with it, or working it out.

- **Reuses:** [[Keepers of the Feather]], Urwin, Sef, the village maps, the reveal itself.
- **Why:** almost no combat prep, maximum interview. Plays to the Lyssa-interrogation strength.
- **Prep needed:** medium.

### Pitch F — **The Second Signet**
> *Two rings. One family. The other one belonged to the champion.*

The unattuned ring and the rune blade taken off the Tomb-Warden, as a premise. The old branch had a founder-champion; his oath-runes held his soul; the party is carrying his sword. **Where else did that family have tombs?** Portable to any domain — this one doesn't need Barovia at all.

- **Reuses:** [[The Harbinger of the Rune-Blade]], the crest and heraldry material, the "blood of the line" door mechanic *(which was the best puzzle of the arc and can be rebuilt anywhere)*.
- **Prep needed:** high — but it's the one with the most room.

## 4. If you run Curse of Strahd

You now have something most CoS tables don't: **three sessions of Barovia already played, on the table, with these players.** Below is what carries over either way, then the two paths.

### What Bad Blood has made canon
- **Lyssa von Zarovich is dead** — permanently, by the party's hand. Strahd's most active internal rival is gone, **and he got it for free.**
- **The old churchyard crypt is open and wrecked** — desecrated, de-consecrated, an emptied ancient prison, a dug shaft, an unsealed dais-elevator surfacing behind the treeline. It is a ready-made dungeon location on the CoS map.
- **Something ancient is loose** — either riding in Lykta or gone to the mountains, depending on your ruling. Amber Temple-adjacent by design.
- **The village survived a siege it should not have.** The Keepers know who did it.
- **Strahd has personally corresponded with four named outsiders and been refused.**
- **The Tarokka reading has never been pulled** and the **Ravenkind Shard was never handed over.** Both of CoS's signature devices are untouched and available.

### Path 1 — same four PCs, straight on
Bad Blood becomes chapter one of the campaign. They're at **Level 8**, in the valley, having refused the door.

- ✅ **The buy-in is already done.** They know the Mists, the village, the Keepers, the Count's methods, and that he uses people. Most CoS tables spend six sessions getting there.
- ✅ **The inciting incident already happened** — the refusal. You don't need a hook; you need a consequence.
- ✅ Lucian's bloodline and Lykta's passenger are two personal arcs you didn't have to invent.
- ⚠️ **Level curve.** CoS is written 1–10 and the party starts at 8. Skip Death House entirely, start around **Vallaki**, and expect to compress the front half. Consider slowing levelling to one per two or three sessions.
- ⚠️ **They already know the twist** — that Strahd manoeuvres outsiders. Lean into it: the campaign question stops being *who is behind this* and becomes ***can you beat someone who is always three moves ahead and does not need to hide it.*** That's a better question anyway.
- ⚠️ Ireena, Izek, the Abbey, Argynvost — none of it has been touched, so the middle of the book is fully intact.

### Path 2 — new party, Bad Blood as history
Run CoS as written; everything above is **backstory the players can discover.**

- The wrecked crypt under the old churchyard is a **dungeon they can enter**, with a dead vampire's laboratory in it and an empty amber prison.
- **The four strangers who went underground during the siege and never came out** become a village legend — and if you ever want them back, they walk out of the Mists as NPCs, or as the *Long Dark* premise fifty years on.
- Lyssa's removal explains why Strahd's grip is currently uncontested.
- **Cameo value:** Ernst Larnak recognises the new party's *type* immediately.
- ✅ No tuning problems. ✅ Death House works. ✅ Full Tarokka reading available.
- ⚠️ You lose the buy-in that's already banked.

### Either way, these carry over unchanged
[[Setting Rules — Barovia]] · [[The Mists — Getting In & Out]] · [[Village of Barovia — Reference]] · [[Dread & Omens]] · [[Ambience & Soundtrack]] · every map (village, church ×3, Castle Ravenloft, outside the village) · every portrait · the **Midjourney house style** · [[Keepers of the Feather]] · the whole **NPC roster**, all of whom are alive.

> [!tip] The honest recommendation
> **Run one more three-shot before committing to CoS.** The format is proven with this group, Pitches A and B are nearly free, and it buys you time to decide whether the Bad Blood party continues. **Curse of Strahd is a year-long commitment; the vault is already built for the short ones.**

## 5. The Bad Blood method — keep doing this

The repeatable process, extracted so the next arc is faster:

1. **Overview + Throughline first.** One page of *what the arc is*, one page of *how the villain causes everything and how the frame works.* Everything else hangs off these two.
2. **A run guide per session** — beat-by-beat with read-aloud in callouts and GM-only in `[!gmonly]`.
3. **★ A Night Sheet per session** — one page: numbers, round scripts, cues, images in order, audio in order, spotlights owed, and a clock. **This is the best thing in the folder.** *Add one thing next time: **level-up triggers as checkboxes**, so a milestone can't be missed live.*
4. **Batch the art** from a single prompt list with a locked house style.
5. **Record it**, then produce three files per session: transcript, **Actual Play** (deviations-first, planned-vs-actual table, carry-forward), **WhatsApp Recap** (player-facing, sent same week).
6. **Update the Tracker after every session** — one page of live state, so prep never starts by re-reading everything.
7. **End every arc with a Conclusion & Continuity doc** — like [[Bad Blood — Arc Conclusion & Continuity]] — so nothing is lost between games.

**Two changes for next time:** prep **half as much** for the final session and leave the space for the table to fill (they will, and it'll be better), and **cut fewer NPCs** — give them smaller jobs instead of removing them, because the ones you cut kept turning up anyway.
