## Recap to open Session 1 *(read aloud — picks up from [[Session 0.5 — Actual Play]]; the same text is embedded in [[Session 1]] Movement 0)*
> [!readaloud]
> Last time: a letter none of you could trace — a job, a road, and a bottle of wine so dark it drank the light. Then a fog that rose the *wrong way*. Lucian kept his head as it swallowed the camp. Dallas threw light into it — and swears he saw faces pulling back from the glow. No stars overhead. Not the same trees. Lykta asked her cards whether to trust the path opening in the mist, and the cards said *yes* — and the wine had turned black as treacle. So you drank it, ash-sweet and sickly, and the fog leaned away in one direction. You left a vial by Jasper's pack with a note — *"if we are not here, drink it and follow"* — and you walked.
>
> The path ended at an iron gate flanked by statues of kings too old to name — and at the thing that guards it. Tall as three of you. Patient. Dragging a runed blade that sang against the stone. Your steel barely slowed it; only radiant light made the runes along that blade gutter. You took the high ground, and it stopped, looked up at you for a long moment, and stepped back into the fog like a servant called away. It never spoke. It never hurried.
>
> Beyond the gate: black pines, a bruise-coloured sky, and a castle on the mountain that watches and never comes closer. A man named Urwin found you in the dark — grieving, plain, and certain, a raven never far from him. He named this land **Barovia** and warned you not to cast your voices beyond the valley: *he* will be listening. Lucian talked him into a contract on the spot — per person, per hour, plus damages — and Urwin, heaven help him, agreed.
>
> He walked you through a village that doesn't come out at night to the only lit building left: a church, its symbol of the dawn worn almost smooth. Father Donovich, white-haired and stubborn, preaching quietly over broken pews. Families sheltering in the undercroft — children pale and too quiet, not enough life in them, two of their number already three days buried. Lykta put a small light into a child's hands and goodberries into the priest's, and the old man wept. Something at the altar caught your eye — you never got the chance to ask. And when Lykta reached out for her whispers, she heard *nothing*: a soul-shaped hole where a presence should be.
>
> Then her scout came back from the dark with the shape of the night: wolves and dead things ringing the cemetery, drifting inward from every side, water finding its level. Urwin whispered to a raven, and the raven flew east. Father Donovich said only: *"Prepare yourselves."*
>
> That was an hour ago — or three days. The Mists keep no honest clock. This is now.

## Recap to open Session 2 *(read aloud — updated 19 July 2026 to match [[Session 1 — Actual Play]]; picks up on the morning after)*
> [!readaloud]
> The church held. Jasper walked out of the Mists alone and found you — a monster hunter with a notebook and a vial of black wine he was too clever to drink — just as the dead came up the graveyard in numbers no one had seen before. The doors exploded. You fought ghouls and dominated wolves and a ghost that came *through the wall*; a dagger thrown through a bottle of holy water; a gnome on a broomstick raining fire — while Lucian held the bell tower alone, all night, eldritch light strobing over the graves. And at the end of it, floating in a dead man's coat: a thing with tentacles where a face should be, that pushed against the inside of your skulls and *lost*. You killed it. In its coat, in a very elegant hand: *"The work at the old churchyard is nearly ready… keep the feather folk busy — and the strangers too. — L."*
>
> But the strangest thing you saw, you worked out yourselves: there were **two armies** out there, and they were fighting *each other* — the dead from the Mists, and wolves and stranger shapes from the tree line — with your church merely in the way. The wolves fought in formation. Dominated. And Urwin, when you asked who commands wolves in this valley, said only: *"We believe Strahd can."* The spawn you killed wore prisoner tattoos. Someone is making servants out of convicts. Someone else is trying to stop her. And nobody asked you to stand in the middle.
>
> A girl named Sef hammered on the barricaded door *after* it was over — safe, furious, and delighted with you — the same girl Jasper hid from in the woods. The survivors of both armies retreated north, into the oldest part of the churchyard, and Lucian watched them go: *"One side is winning."* You barred the doors, took what rest Barovia allows, and grew stronger for it.
>
> Morning has come — a brighter grey. The battlefield is waiting to be read, Father Donovich has promised Lykta a walk among the family crests, and somewhere past the north gate, under the leaning stones of the old quarter, something is still down there. This is where we begin.

## Recap to open Session 3 *(SUPERSEDED 21 July — S2 as played ended mid-cliffhanger; the live recap is embedded in [[Session 3]] Movement 1: "Roll initiative." The version below assumed the pre-play S2 shape — kept for reference only)*
> [!readaloud]
> The battlefield told you the church was never the target — just the obstacle. The crests told you whose graves you'd been sleeping over: **von Zarovich** — cadet lines, cousins, the family's quiet dead — the same tree as the seal on the letter that brought you here. A different branch. And in the old quarter, behind the black yews, you found where the diggers went: a mausoleum with a door that has no lock — only a basin, a carved thorn-vine, and an instruction: *"The tree drinks only of its own."* *(If Lucian's blood opened it:)* It drank from Lucian — and it **knew** him. The door knelt. Nobody has said the obvious thing out loud yet. *(If they broke in through the shaft:)* You went in the diggers' way — through the shaft, like thieves — and the crypt knew it.
>
> Below: a family being erased from its own graves. Every nameplate chiselled blank. Coffins hauled *down*, deeper, toward green candlelight. Lyssa's dig crew dead where the Warden left them — in two pieces, one stroke each — and one broken survivor, Milos, who finally gave the enemy a name: **"The Lady. Lyssa. Von Zarovich — like the Count. She laughed when she said it."** The last of her field-test force went down fighting among the false coffins. *(If the wight was spared:)* You showed the archer mercy. The Warden didn't.
>
> And then the grinding came down the stair, and the thing from the gate cornered you in a rotunda where its own toppled statue lay broken on the floor — the Tomb-Warden, runes screaming, come for the blood that opened the door. *(If ended by radiant:)* You killed it. The runes guttered out one by one, and for a heartbeat, as the last one died, they resolved into something older — the same warding tradition as a wax seal you've seen before. *(If only driven off:)* You broke it — but not with the light, and things like that don't stay broken here.
>
> Now you stand at the sealed stair — a great slab carved with the raven and the sun, its wards unmade, levered aside with engines. Below: a spiral of green light, hundreds of cold candles, something breathing with too many lungs — and a voice you've never matched to a face, clear and amused, drifting up: *"Do come down. I so rarely get guests worth the candles."*
>
> You are stronger than you have ever been. You're going to need to be.

## Aftermath — how the world reacts to what they did
*Drop these in so choices echo. Adjust to your table's outcomes.*

### From Session 1 *(as played — 13 July)*
- **Sef is alive and was never in danger.** She arrived post-siege; her bond with the party is fascination, not rescue-gratitude — and she and Jasper have a *"you hid from ME?"* double-act. Urwin is openly fond of the party.
- **The Brauns and the sheltered families made it.** The village quietly leaves bread and candles for the party. One defender went down and was stabilised — he's awake, arm in a sling, embarrassingly grateful. The children below are *slightly* brighter. Not all of them.
- **The church held cleanly** — the Keepers are confident allies, and Urwin helped all night as a human. **The wereraven reveal is still in pocket.**
- **The party's three theories are all correct** (two related bloodlines; servants made from prisoners; Strahd controls wolves) — keep rewarding them.

### From Session 2 *(the crypt — updated for the 19 July rewrite)*
- **Whose blood opened the door:** if it was Lucian's, the tomb — and by S3, **Lyssa** — knows a child of the line walked in. Donovich looked at him a half-second too long and said nothing. The table's arithmetic is its own aftermath; don't help it.
- **Milos:** if sent up, he's by Donovich's fire, flinching at bells, kneeling when Lucian passes. A surviving witness for S3 and beyond.
- **The wight shown mercy:** found bisected on the entry stair. Nobody says anything. (If they killed it themselves, its silvered arrows serve Jasper well.)
- **Harbinger ended vs driven off:** ended by radiant → Lykta felt the *release*, a sentry relieved of its post, and the Keepers speak of the party in a different register. Driven off → it re-forms and watches the churchyard in S3 (see the complication note there).
- **Grave-goods taken:** the Keepers won't touch them. *"Sell them outside, if outside will have you."*

### From Session 3 *(rewritten 21–26 July — two endings, one epilogue)*
- **★ They fell (the likely branch): the Long Dark.** Fifty years. Every NPC thread resolves off-screen: Urwin dead twenty years; Donovich buried by his altar; the Keepers scattered to a handful under **Sef, 65**, who spent her life on this and paid the Amber Temple's price. Lyssa — dead in the Unchaining, or broken by the Count after their deaths (per the table). The party wakes with **hidden Dark Gifts** (nothing revealed at the table) and their four dying thoughts — spoken at the Fall, read back fifty years old — **and they died knowing whose errand it was: Lyssa told them at the Trap.** The next campaign opens at the graves, on a party that is owed.
- **They stood (the miracle branch):** Lyssa dead in the Unchaining or fled diminished; the lab is theirs (notes, Gem of Brightness, gold); the Mists open on the eastern road — **no coach, no letter, no explanation from *him*, and none needed:** they know from Lyssa's own mouth that Strahd fetched them, and the open door is the payment. Lykta's raven-mail is never answered — and now she knows what the silence meant. **Strahd in person stays unseen until a sequel earns him.**
- **They retreated mid-session:** entirely legitimate — Level 8 (if the Warden fell), a long rest, the Boost Kit at last, and [[Session 4]] becomes the re-planned assault.
- **The seal below** *(the sarcophagus — updated 25 Jul)*: in nearly every branch it **opens** — the Unchaining. What keeps for the sequel is the **empty cell**, the freed Choir roosting in the amber, and the question of what else her notes woke.

### Into the ending
- The personal stakes and the larger pattern (the digs, the faction war) both converge on the door home — so the party reaches the finale *invested*, not just hired.
