If they stand: the Mists open — and they go home knowing *exactly* whose game they won, told by his rival, never by him.

> [!gmonly] The lethality contract — read this twice
> Every prior session had a floor: the wereraven rescue, the Mists-swallow net, "Strahd won't let his tools die." **Tonight the floors are OFF.** Strahd's protection was always about *usefulness* — and by tonight, win or lose, the party has already done their job: Lyssa's dig is exposed, her crew is dead, her warden-problem is loose in her stairwell. If they die in her Trap, that outcome suits the Count *fine* — she spends her strength killing them and he crushes what's left at leisure. Run both fights with real teeth: legendaries on, Mind Burst on clumps, focus-fire the healer if that's what the creatures would do. Kill with a straight face and full dignity — no gloating, no apology. The epilogue is not a consolation prize; it's the best scene in the arc.
>
> **The one mercy that stands:** if the party *retreats* — back up the dug hole, out of the crypt, to Urwin — let them go. Level 8 (if the Warden fell), the Boost Kit, a long rest, and a re-planned assault next session are all legitimate. The trap only kills people who walk into it. If they do this, tonight ends early and the finale moves; that's the players playing well, not the plan failing.

Not in this."*), and *"we have you"* — twice, fifty years apart. Give her the room's quiet moments; she is carrying the arc's soul.
> - **Dallas (Glen) — ★ NEW: the vats are his.** The brine vats are alchemically unstable (Bluetspur solution). **Any fire or acid damage ruptures a vat: 10-ft burst, DC 15 Dex save, 3d6 acid — and vats within 10 ft of a rupturing vat chain.** The acid gnome who deleted a room with one Fireball in S2 gets to melt her production line on-screen — and every rupture drags Lyssa's fury (and focus) onto him for a round; being *hated by the villain for the right reasons* is the spotlight. Colour to keep: near the seal his skeletal Mage Hand manifests… kneeling, facing the sarcophagus. During the Unchaining, for one round his magic burns clean — and faintly **amber**. Say nothing. *(The gift, seeded early.)*
> - **Jasper (David) — ★ NEW: the hunter's night.** (1) **The Warded Line:** with Protection from Evil and Good running, the Warden (undead) has Disadvantage on attacks against him and its Dread of the Tomb can't touch him — **the one man who can stand between it and Lucian.** Rule generously when he interposes: a Reaction to swap himself into an attack aimed at an adjacent ally is exactly the veteran move he's been saving all arc. (2) **Hunter's Eye:** the first time he engages or studies a vampire mind flayer, the notebook pays off — hand him the tells (the Mind Burst telegraph; the grapple → Drink Sapience kill-chain) and give the party **Advantage on their first save against each ability he calls out loud.** The monster hunter meets a monster nobody has ever written down — and starts the page.

> [!gmonly] ★ THE CHOIR BELOW — the GM truth that ties the whole arc (read before the table) ★
> **What it is.** At the far end of the sanctuary, sealed in a great black **sarcophagus** bricked into the wall, is a **vestige** — the remnant of something dead and divine, the same order of being as the things that sleep in the **Amber Temple**. It speaks as *we* — call it **the Choir Below** (its true name is yours, and tonight it never gives one). It has been down here longer than the Zarovich name.
> **Why the old branch existed.** Lucian's mother's line — the *older branch*, the erased names, the sun rosettes, the Tomb-Warden's oath — were its **jailers**. That was the family's purpose: consecrated ground above, anchor-stones below, a warden at the stair. Strahd's histories remember them as rivals; the truth is they were the lock on a door he never knew mattered. *(This is why the blood door opens on Lucian's blood, why the Warden's oath "ends at the seal," and why the names were worth erasing.)*
> **What Lyssa did.** She found her family's prison and turned it into a **battery farm**. The de-consecration upstairs (S2's erasure — the party already proved it) stripped the outer wards; down here she inverted the rest: the **green candles are chains** — each flame a stolen scrap of the Choir, siphoned through the **anchor-stones** and fed into her work. The Bluetspur tadpoles supply the *bodies*; the Choir supplies the *dark magic* that makes her new servants possible. **Without it, the project dies. Permanently.**
> **Why the voices called Lykta.** Fifty years of screaming and nobody heard. Then a **spirit medium** walked into the valley — the first ear that *could*. *"Come to us"* was never bait and never the spawn: it was a prisoner, calling to the one person who might hear, who happened to travel with **the last of the jailers' blood** — the one key left in the world. The Choir arranged nothing; it only *called*. But it knows exactly who is coming down the stairs, and it has had fifty years to think about what to say.
> **What it wants.** Out. Not worship, not souls, not (tonight) revenge — *out*. It is not good; it is a dead god's leftover and the next campaign can decide how monstrous. But tonight its interests and the party's point the same direction, and it is scrupulously honest with Lykta — a fact that should itself be unsettling.
> **Where it goes.** Freed, it pours out of the deep dark and is gone — **toward the mountains.** To the Amber Temple, where its kind roost. Fifty years later, when an old wereraven walks in and asks for four dead strangers by name, **the thing that answers already knows them** — *"we have you"* was a promise kept. That is why the bargain was possible at all. (Sef still pays the Temple's own price — the toll is for the door, not the souls.)
> **The one-line version for running it:** *the voices are real, they are underneath everything, Lykta is the ear, Lucian is the key, the candles are the chains — and breaking them is the blow.*

# MOVEMENT 3 — The Green Passage *(15 minutes — dread, no combat)*

▸ **Goal:** the walk to the Trap. The voices get faces. Nobody attacks — that's what makes it terrible.

🖼 *(no scene image — Area B is on the table; uncover it as they cross the seam)*

📖 *(through the great doors)*

> [!readaloud]
> Beyond the door the dark is full of small green flames — candles, hundreds of them, burning the colour of pond-ice and giving no heat at all. And as your light finds the walls you see where they're standing: **grave-niches.** Row upon row of them, floor to ceiling down a descending passage — the oldest dead of the line, coffins of black wood and lead shrouded in dust, each niche with a candle burning at its lip like an offering. This is the deep crypt — the tomb *beneath* the tomb, where the family kept its first dead. The nameplates down here are chiselled blank, same as above. Somebody erased them where they slept.
>
> The air is cold, old, and *interested.* And standing among the graves, spaced like an honour guard, are the voices.
>
> They were people. They are beautiful now, in the way a knife is — pale, still, eyes catching the candlelight. None of them moves toward you. One inclines its head, courteous as a footman, and speaks with a mouth that has forgotten how: *"She is expecting you."*
>
> And — Lykta — you hear it before you can name it. The whisper comes again as you pass: *come to us.* But the sentinel's lips move **half a beat behind the words.** Like a poor puppet. Like something being *worn.* The voice was never theirs. # MOVEMENT 7 — ★ THE LONG DARK *(the true epilogue — 25 minutes, protected)* ★

▸ **Goal:** fifty years in one breath; four shallow graves; one very old woman. The campaign's ending and the next campaign's cold open, in the same scene. **Reveal almost nothing about what they now are.**

🖼 ![[Scene - The Long Dark.webp|500]]

> [!readaloud]

>

>

>

🎲 **Play the reunion small and human.** She helps them out of the mud one at a time. She counts them — *"…three, four. Good"* — and her voice breaks on *good*, the only crack she allows. It said —"* and here her voice does something strange, because she is quoting it exactly, *"—* 'we have them.' *And we agreed on a price."* *(that's ALL she says. If pressed: "You'll know what I paid when you need to. Not tonight.")* **★ GM truth — the knot that ties the arc:** the thing that answered is **the Choir Below**, roosted among its kind in the amber. *"We have you"* was a promise of custody: it caught their souls the night they fell and held them out of the valley's reach — out of the Count's reach — for fifty years, waiting for someone to come asking. The bargain was possible **because they freed it.** The debt ran *toward* them. Sef's price was the Temple's toll for the door — not for the souls; those, the Choir gave back willingly. **The players learn none of this tonight.** Lykta may notice only this: the dead of the graveyard are whispering again, all around — and every whisper is *polite* to her.
- **★ If they ask about the Count** *(they know, now — Lyssa told them before the end)*: she goes flat. *"Not a word. Not in fifty years. He got what he wanted the night you died, and he never so much as marked your graves — the Keepers did that."* *(beat)* *"You were his brooms. Remember that, when you decide what to do with being alive."* — the sequel's first ember, laid by hand.
- **Why:** *"Because you held a door for people you'd never met."* *(beat)* *"And because nobody else was ever going to come for you."*

> [!gmonly] ★ THE HIDDEN GIFTS — reveal NOTHING at the table ★
> Each PC carries a Dark Gift from the Amber Temple's vestiges — the price of four souls — and **the players learn none of it tonight.** No mechanics, no names, no descriptions. At most, ONE sensory wrongness each, delivered flat and never explained; discovery belongs to the next campaign. A menu (pick/replace freely — decide the actual gifts between campaigns, not now):
> - **Lucian:** his reflection in a puddle is a half-second behind him. *(Say nothing else.)*
> - **Lykta:** silence. For the first time since childhood, the dead aren't whispering — or rather, they've stopped talking *about* her and started *waiting for orders.* Tonight it just feels like quiet.
> - **Dallas:** he cups a hand out of habit and the light he conjures comes out **amber**, and warm, and doesn't flicker in the rain. He didn't cast anything.
> - **Jasper:** every scar he ever had is gone — including the ones he was fond of. Under his ribs, something keeps time that isn't quite a heartbeat. His old notebook, buried with him, is dry.
> **Sef's price (GM-only, hold it):** she cannot change any more. The vestige took her wings — a wereraven who will never fly again, standing under trees full of ravens. If a player ever asks why she doesn't just *shift*… that's a next-campaign scene.

🎲 **Return the last thoughts.** Read each character's dying thought back to them from your notes — fifty years old now — and have the player answer with one line about opening their eyes. That's the character's first breath of the new campaign.

📖 *(the close)*

> [!readaloud]
> The rain eases. Down the slope, where the village was, a handful of lights still burn — fewer than you remember, but burning. And high on its mountain, patient as ever, the castle sits exactly where you left it, exactly as it has sat for four hundred years, and one window — the same window — is lit.
>
> Fifty years, and the valley never even noticed you were gone. Sef shoulders the spade like a woman half her age and looks back at the four of you, mud-born and breathing.
>
> "Come on, then," she says. "You've been dead half a century. There's work."

> [!example] CLOSE THE ARC — the Long Dark
> - **Cut there.** No mechanics, no levelling, no gift reveals, no questions answered. The next campaign opens on this exact spot.
> - **What carries forward (GM ledger):** four hidden Dark Gifts · Sef's unpaid-for price · **the Choir Below — freed, roosting in the amber, holding a debt it considers PAID and an acquaintance it considers OPEN** (it likes Lykta; that should worry everyone) · Lyssa — *dead in the Unchaining, or* diminished-not-dead, per the table · the Harbinger — **released in every branch where the Binding broke** (by radiant, or by the Unchaining taking his chains with all the others); only if the Binding somehow never broke does he still walk the empty crypt (sequel gift) · the east wall below — **open and empty now**, and the question of what else her notes woke · Lykta's letter, never answered — *Strahd knows they wrote, and Strahd knows they're back* · the Amber Temple, which the Choir now calls home.
> - **Strahd in person: still never seen.** The *frame* is known — his rival named it at the Trap, truthfully, and fifty years of silence have confirmed it — but the man himself has never once appeared, written, or spoken. When he finally does, it will be worth it: the party comes back **owed**.
> - Debrief the table gently — a TPK that becomes a cold open needs five minutes of "that was the plan, you magnificent doomed idiots" honesty. Then pack up.
